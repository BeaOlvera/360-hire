## 3. Intended purpose — and the limits of it

### 3.1 What the system is for

Assisting trained recruiters and hiring managers in assessing candidates for a defined role. It gathers structured evidence and produces a **recommendation for a human decision-maker**.

### 3.2 What the system is not for

| Not for | Why |
|---|---|
| Making hiring decisions | It produces a recommendation. A human must decide. |
| Automated screening or shortlisting | No candidate may be rejected, filtered or ranked out without a person acting. This is both a design property and a legal requirement (GDPR Art. 22). |
| Assessing candidates for a role other than the one they applied for | Purpose limitation. Reuse requires a fresh basis and fresh notice. |
| Producing a general employability or "talent" score reused across contexts | Approaches the Art. 5(1)(c) prohibition on social scoring. |
| Inferring emotional state, honesty, or mental health | The system does not do this and must not be described as doing it. |
| Use by people without recruitment or assessment training | See §7. |

### 3.3 The single most important instruction

**The recommendation is an opinion generated by a language model. It is not a measurement.**

Treat `strong_hire` / `hire` / `maybe` / `no_hire` as you would treat a colleague's written impression after an interview: informative, worth weighing, and not authoritative. The full transcript is appended to every comprehensive report specifically so that you can check any claim against what the candidate actually said. **Check it.** A reviewer who reads only the summary is not exercising oversight in the sense Art. 14 requires.

## 4. Capabilities, limitations and expected accuracy

### 4.1 Accuracy

**No accuracy metric has yet been established for this system.** Testing against defined metrics is scheduled but not complete (see RMS-ZC-HIRE-2026-001 §5). Until it is, no claim about the system's accuracy should be made to candidates, to clients, or internally.

This is stated plainly because the alternative — an unsupported number — would be worse.

### 4.2 Known limitations

| # | Limitation | What to do about it |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Verbal fluency inflates competency scores.** Candidates assessed in a second language, and some neurodivergent candidates, are systematically disadvantaged. | Offer the candidate their stronger language (EN or ES). When reviewing, ask whether a low score reflects competence or expression. |
| 2 | **The reasoning instrument carries adverse-impact risk.** Cognitive tests have well-documented subgroup differences. | Enable it only where the role genuinely requires it, and record why. Do not enable it by default. |
| 3 | The model can mischaracterise or over-summarise an incident. | Read the appended transcript for any competency that materially affects your decision. |
| 4 | CV extraction can fail or mis-extract, especially for non-PDF or unusual layouts. | The Skip path must remain available. A missing CV is not a candidate failure. |
| 5 | Anti-fabrication probes raise the cost of invented answers; they do **not** detect deception. | Never describe or treat any output as a judgement about honesty. |
| 6 | Video upload is best-effort and can fail on long sessions. | A missing or partial recording is a technical outcome, never evidence about the candidate. |
| 7 | Instruments are public-domain adaptations, not the validated commercial originals. | Do not present results as clinically or commercially validated scores. |

## 5. Human oversight (Article 14)

### 5.1 What the system requires of you

| Requirement | Practice |
|---|---|
| A named person exercises oversight | Assign it. Record who. |
| That person has the competence to do so | Trained in recruitment and assessment; understands §4.2 |
| That person has authority to override | They can disregard the recommendation without needing approval |
| Oversight occurs before the decision | Review happens before any communication to the candidate |
| Automation bias is actively resisted | See §5.2 |

### 5.2 Resisting automation bias

Art. 14(4)(b) specifically requires deployers to remain aware of the tendency to over-rely on automated output. Two practical measures:

1. **Form your own view before opening the recommendation.** Read the transcript first. Then read what the system concluded. Where you disagree, your reasoning is what goes in the file.
2. **Track your override rate.** If it is near zero across many candidates, oversight has probably become nominal. A reviewer who never disagrees is not reviewing.

### 5.3 What must never be automated

Do not connect this system's output to any downstream process that acts without a person. No auto-rejection, no threshold cut-off, no automatic ranking into a shortlist. If you want this, it is a change of legal classification, not a configuration option — talk to the provider first.

## 8. Configuration guidance

| Setting | Guidance |
|---|---|
| Which instruments to enable | The fewest the role justifies. The per-candidate customisation feature exists to **reduce** the default set, not to expand it. Every instrument you enable is personal data you must justify. |
| The reasoning instrument | Off unless the role requires it, with the reason recorded. |
| Video recording | Consider whether you need it. The transcript carries the evidence; the recording adds visibility of the candidate's appearance, which introduces bias risk without adding assessment value. |
| Language | Offer the candidate the choice. Do not default to the employer's language. |
| Competency weighting | Critical / Important / Relevant should reflect the actual job, and should be set before any candidate is assessed, not after. |
